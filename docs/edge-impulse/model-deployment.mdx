# Model Testing & Deployment

Once your model is trained, you need to verify it actually works before deploying it to the board. This section covers how to evaluate your model's performance and get it running on the Arduino Q.

---

## Step 1: Train the Model

1. In Edge Impulse Studio, go to **Classifier** (or your learning block).
2. Click **Start training**.
3. Wait for training to complete: this usually takes 1–3 minutes for small datasets.

After training, Edge Impulse shows you:

- **Accuracy**: percentage of training samples correctly classified.
- **Loss**: how far off the predictions were on average (lower is better).
- **Confusion matrix**: a breakdown of which classes were confused with each other.

A good starting target is **85%+ accuracy** on the training set. If it's lower, you may need more data or better-separated classes.

---

## Step 2: Test the Model

Training accuracy alone is not enough: the model might be overfitting (memorising training data rather than learning patterns). Use the **Test set** to get an honest evaluation.

1. Go to **Model testing** in Edge Impulse Studio.
2. Click **Classify all**.
3. Review the results:
   - **Test accuracy**: how well the model performs on data it has never seen.
   - **Confusion matrix**: identifies which classes are being misclassified.

If test accuracy is significantly lower than training accuracy, your model is likely overfitting. Try collecting more varied data or reducing model complexity.

---

## Step 3: Live Classification

Test in Edge Impulse Studio before you attach the model to the board.

1. Go to **Live classification** in Edge Impulse Studio.
2. Use your **laptop camera** or **phone** (QR code) as the input. You do not need `edge-impulse-daemon` for this bootcamp image flow.
3. Trigger each class (for example step into frame, then step out).
4. Watch the confidence scores in real time.

This is the fastest way to spot label or lighting problems before you use the model on the UNO Q.

---

## Step 4: Attach the model in Arduino App Lab

On the UNO Q, inference runs on the **Linux side** through an App Lab **Brick** (Python), not inside the Arduino sketch. Do **not** export an **Arduino library** `.zip`, and do **not** use **Sketch → Include Library → Add .ZIP Library**. That zip is for classic MCU boards in Arduino IDE.

The board flow is: sign in to Edge Impulse from App Lab → pick the model on the Brick → Download → Brick Configuration.

1. Open your App in **Arduino App Lab** (or import a starter App).
2. Click **Add Brick** if needed, and add the matching AI Brick. For a live USB camera classifier, that is **Video Image Classification**.
3. Click the Brick, then open the **AI models** tab.
4. Sign in with your **Arduino** account, then **Connect to Edge Impulse**.
5. Choose a model from the list, or click **Train new AI model** to open Studio, train, and come back.
6. Click **Download** on the model so App Lab installs it on the UNO Q.
7. Open **Brick Configuration**, select that model, and click **Save**.

App Lab writes the model name into `app.yaml`. You do not copy a `.eim` path by hand.

In Python, the Brick runs the model. Your `sketch.ino` only handles actuators over the **Bridge** (LED, buzzer, and so on).

:::tip
Arduino library vs App Lab
Keep an **App** `.zip` only when you are importing a whole App Lab project. An Edge Impulse **Arduino library** `.zip` is a different file and will not attach the model to a Brick.
:::
